ShipLogic is the fulfillment arm of American Exchange Group, operating as a 3PL for brands within the same holding company — with stated interest in serving external 3PL clients. The Perris, California facility (~500,000 sq ft) has been live on Deposco since mid-2025, following a move from a former Riverside location; some legacy-system business continues to run alongside Deposco in the same building. The Somerset, New Jersey facility has not yet gone live on Deposco — a QuickBooks Online socket was enabled in July 2026 in preparation for that launch.

B2B is the operational center of gravity. It accounts for roughly 31% of outbound shipments by count but approximately 92% of unit volume, driven by large retailer purchase orders with high carton quantities per shipment. D2C is stable and growing, with the Aerosoles brand going live in August 2026 without reported issues.

Bulk-Pick Flow

Not used for all B2B orders — some orders are waved directly against standard storage locations.

  1. 1 ShipLogic manually assigns an order group code to the set of orders to process together.
  2. 2 Custom action creates replenishment tasks for the total inventory needed across the group; pallets auto-generated with labels.
  3. 3 Users bulk-pick one SKU at a time and stage pallets.
  4. 4 Staged pallets move into a dedicated production picking zone — each SKU in a separate location.
  5. 5 Orders waved against zone inventory; users batch-pick each store order into separate bins.
  6. 6 Packing and shipping flow varies by parcel vs. freight.
Parcel Packing
  1. 1 Single Scan Packing packs bins into UCC-labeled cartons. Shipping label is generated but not printed.
  2. 2 Single Container Packing scans UCC cartons, closes the shipment, and prints the shipping label — ensuring each label is matched to its carton.
Freight Store Packing
  1. 1 Single Scan Packing creates UCC cartons without creating a shipment.
  2. 2 Build Pallet assembles cartons to pallets and generates pallet labels and SSCC numbers.
  3. 3 Assign Trip to Container stages pallets at dock door; a subsequent process hard-closes the shipment when freight departs.
Freight Case Fulfillment
  1. 1 Standard single-order waving against reserve storage locations.
  2. 2 Single Scan Packing — case packs are picked to a pallet or pallets.
  3. 3 Print 2 Panda — UCC labels are generated and printed for every case pack.
  4. 4 Assign Trip to Container stages pallets at dock door; a subsequent process hard-closes the shipment when freight departs.
Master Carton Parcel Packing
  1. 1 Single Scan Packing packs bins into UCC-labeled cartons. Shipping label is generated but not printed.
  2. 2 Build Pallet — UCC-labeled cartons are packed into larger shipping cartons.
  3. 3 Assign Trip to Container — master shipping cartons are shipped. Parcel shipping integrations generate and print shipping labels for each master carton.
Freight Routing
1 Routing team captures weights and dimensions, sends required details to the retailer, and updates orders with load information.
2 Creates a trip (custom action); trip number stored in Order Header Custom Attribute 4. Pallets assigned to dock door.
3 Prints master BOLs for the trip and individual BOLs by load number.
System Challenges
  • B2B Parcel Fulfillment Workflows
    B2B parcel workflows differ fundamentally from D2C — streamlined processes like Pack From Container cannot be applied, requiring purpose-built workflows for each fulfillment type. At scale, there are few options to batch or accelerate processing: large orders and groups of orders with many cartons must be individually processed through each step — packing, UCC labeling, and shipment creation — before a shipping label can be generated. As order sizes and volumes grow, the per-carton overhead compounds quickly.
  • B2B Item Attribute Tracking — Stock Marked for Specific Retailers
    ShipLogic carries SKUs where physically distinct units share the same UPC and item record — the differences arise because that stock was manufactured specifically for a particular retail partner. Orders for that retailer must be fulfilled from the correct pool; fulfilling from the wrong stock risks shipping non-compliant product. The current workaround uses Deposco's lot tracking functionality, with lot numbers set to retailer codes to associate the right stock with the right orders. While functional, this repurposes a mechanism not designed for this use case — and overriding lot-based controls when inventory is tight or an order needs to ship immediately adds friction that operators must navigate carefully.
  • B2B Freight Trip Routing, Planning, and Shipping Workflow Alignment
    The freight routing process spans multiple manual steps and team handoffs — capturing carton weights and dimensions, coordinating retailer routing instructions, creating trips in Deposco, assigning pallets to dock doors, and producing BOLs. Keeping Deposco's order and shipment data synchronized with retailer EDI requirements and internal routing decisions is a persistent challenge, particularly for multi-load trips and shipments spanning multiple orders.
  • Reserve Storage Access & Management
    All reserve storage at the Perris facility is in very-narrow-aisle (VNA) racking, accessible only with turret trucks operated by certified personnel. This constraint affects every task type that interacts with reserve locations: putaway tasks following inbound receipts, replenishment tasks moving stock from reserve to forward pick locations, and direct pick tasks for large B2B case-fulfillment orders. Each of these requires matching work to an available VNA-qualified operator and equipment — a scheduling dependency that does not exist for standard locations, and a potential throughput bottleneck when VNA resources are in high demand across multiple concurrent task types.
  • Replenishments — Storage Management and Replenishment Execution
    With a large and diverse SKU assortment across a ~500,000 sq ft facility, managing forward pick storage locations at scale is a persistent operational challenge. Determining which SKUs warrant dedicated forward pick locations, how much capacity to allocate per SKU, and keeping those locations stocked at the right quantities requires continuous attention as order velocity and product mix shift across brands and channels. As the active SKU count grows, maintaining an organized and efficiently stocked forward pick face becomes increasingly complex.
  • Fragmented B2B Workflows with Difficult-to-Follow SOPs
    ShipLogic operates multiple distinct B2B fulfillment workflows side-by-side — bulk-pick parcel, freight store packing, freight case fulfillment, and master carton parcel packing — each with its own sequence of picking, packing, and shipping steps. Without strong in-system routing or workflow enforcement, directing operators to the correct procedure relies on training and informal SOPs. As workflow variants multiply, maintaining consistency, reducing execution errors, and onboarding new operators efficiently becomes increasingly difficult.
